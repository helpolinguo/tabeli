%%K t14-tit-1 sub
\VUpk{11.4pt}{40}{The Street. --- The Tradespeople.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t14-01-1 p
1. --- My friend John, who lives in the country, has come to spend three days with my family.
\VUgras{Until} now he had never had the chance to see a big town, so we are spending all our
days walking about and I explain to him whatever he does not know.

%%K t14-02-1 p
2. --- First we went to see the \VUgras{observatory} \textsuperscript{(1)}, which interested
him greatly. Coming back along the \VUgras{street} \textsuperscript{(3)} where the
\VUgras{Turkish baths} \textsuperscript{(2)} are, we met a \VUgras{funeral}
\textsuperscript{(4)}. At the head of the \VUgras{funeral procession} walked the
\VUgras{clergy}, ahead of the \VUgras{hearse} in which the \VUgras{coffin} had
been placed. Many friends followed the family in \VUgras{mourning}.

%%K t14-02-2 p
After the procession had passed, we crossed the street in front of the big \VUgras{brasserie}
\textsuperscript{(5)}, where many \VUgras{customers} were drinking \VUgras{beer} on the
terrace, sheltered by the glazed
\VUgras{awning} \textsuperscript{(6)}.

%%K t14-03-1 p
3. --- John was puzzled by the \VUgras{coat of arms} set between the shop of the
\VUgras{wine and spirits merchant} \textsuperscript{(7)} and that of the
\VUgras{music seller} \textsuperscript{(10)}. He asked me what it meant; I told him it
marked the English \VUgras{consulate} \textsuperscript{(8)}, and pointed out the
\VUgras{consul} \textsuperscript{(9)} standing on the balcony.

%%K t14-tit-2 sub
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{Looking at the shops.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t14-04-1 p
4. --- Naturally we stopped in front of the display of
\VUgras{musical instruments}, \VUgras{harmoniums}, \VUgras{organs} and
\VUgras{pianos}; we looked at the illustrated covers of the \VUgras{scores}
and the piano \VUgras{pieces}, and admired the gilded wooden \VUgras{lyre} used as a shop
sign.

%%K t14-05-1 p
5. --- The clouds of dust raised by the \VUgras{street sweepers} \textsuperscript{(11)} and
the \VUgras{sweeping machine} \textsuperscript{(12)} forced us to hurry past the fine
\VUgras{suites of furniture} at the
\VUgras{furniture dealer's} \textsuperscript{(13)} and past the display of
\VUgras{stoves} and \VUgras{lamps} at the \VUgras{ironmonger's} \textsuperscript{(14)}.

%%K t14-06-1 p
6. --- At that spot we did have to leave the pavement, because it was blocked by parcels being
taken off a \VUgras{removal van} \textsuperscript{(16)} to be carried into a \VUgras{shop}
\textsuperscript{(15)} that had just been let.

%%K t14-06-2 p
That stopped us seeing the fine carriages at the
\VUgras{coachbuilder's} \textsuperscript{(17)}, but not from stopping to watch the
\VUgras{packer} \textsuperscript{(19)} making a crate for his neighbour, the
\VUgras{cycle and car dealer} \textsuperscript{(18)}. Then, as it was already rather
late, we went home.

%%K t14-tit-3 sub
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{A walk through the town.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t14-07-1 p
7. --- The next day my mother suggested that John should have his photograph taken, and asked
me to go with him to the
\VUgras{photographer's} \textsuperscript{(20)}. After lunch we went to the artist's
\VUgras{studio}, where we had to wait a good while. To pass the time we
looked at the \VUgras{portraits} \textsuperscript{(22)} hanging on the wall.

%%K t14-07-2 p
When our turn came it did not take long, and as soon as my friend had finished posing in front
of the \VUgras{camera} \textsuperscript{(21)}, we went back down.

%%K t14-08-1 p
8. --- On the first floor we saw, through the open door of the
\VUgras{hairdresser's} \textsuperscript{(23)}, his assistant cutting a customer's hair.

%%K t14-08-2 p
Out in the street again, we passed the \VUgras{tobacconist's} \textsuperscript{(24)}. John was
so amused by the red \VUgras{lantern} \textsuperscript{(25)} and the
\VUgras{big pipe} used as a \VUgras{sign} \textsuperscript{(26)} that he bumped into two
old gentlemen chatting over a \VUgras{pinch of snuff} \textsuperscript{(27)}.

%%K t14-tit-4 sub
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{The cake shop.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t14-09-1 p
9. --- The \VUgras{banknotes} and \VUgras{coins} of every country displayed in the
\VUgras{window} of the \VUgras{money changer's} \textsuperscript{(29)} held our attention for
a moment, but as it was teatime we went into the
\VUgras{pastry cook's} \textsuperscript{(31)} without stopping any longer.

%%K t14-10-1 p
10. --- At the sight of all the \VUgras{good things} the shop held ---
\VUgras{cakes, gingerbread, biscuits} and \VUgras{sweets} of every kind --- we
could hardly choose. In the end John settled on the \VUgras{cream cakes}, and I took only a
small \VUgras{cherry tart}, because sweet things
\VUgras{give me toothache} and I would rather not visit the
\VUgras{dentist} \textsuperscript{(30)} too often.

%%K t14-tit-5 sub
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{Typewriting.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t14-11-1 p
11. --- To show my friend a \VUgras{typewriter}, which he had heard of but never seen, we went
into the \VUgras{office} \textsuperscript{(32)} of my
\VUgras{cousin} \textsuperscript{(34)}. We found him dictating his letters to his \VUgras{secretary}
\textsuperscript{(35)}, who is a \VUgras{shorthand writer}. A letter was hardly finished
before a \VUgras{typist} \textsuperscript{(33)} copied it out on his machine. We did not want
to interrupt his work, so we only stayed a few minutes.

%%K t14-12-1 p
12. --- Below my cousin's office lives a great
\VUgras{clockmaker and jeweller} \textsuperscript{(37)} who is a silversmith as well.
His splendid shop holds many \VUgras{clocks, mantel clocks, watches},
\VUgras{chains} and costly \VUgras{jewels}, such as \VUgras{pearl necklaces},
gold \VUgras{bracelets}, \VUgras{earrings} and \VUgras{rings} set with
\VUgras{precious stones} and \VUgras{diamonds}. One of the windows is given
over entirely to \VUgras{silverware} and holds a large collection of silver
\VUgras{cutlery}.

%%K t14-13-1 p
13. --- Turning the corner of the street, I noticed that the
\VUgras{nameplate} \textsuperscript{(36)} beside the \VUgras{number} of the house had been
changed. I was about to say so out loud when we heard the cheerful sound of a military band.
We began to run towards the regiment, without thinking that it was going to pass in front of
the shops of the \VUgras{hatter} \textsuperscript{(39)} and the \VUgras{glover}
\textsuperscript{(40)}, and that we would have been just as well placed to watch it march past
by staying in front of the window of the \VUgras{optician} \textsuperscript{(38)}, full of
\VUgras{pince-nez, spectacles} and \VUgras{opera glasses}.

%%K t14-tit-6 sub
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{The march past.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t14-14-1 p
14. --- At the head of the \VUgras{regiment} \textsuperscript{(41)} marched the
\VUgras{drum major} \textsuperscript{(42)}, followed by the \VUgras{drummers}
\textsuperscript{(43)}, the \VUgras{buglers} \textsuperscript{(44)} and the whole
\VUgras{band}. The
\VUgras{general} \textsuperscript{(45)}, who was on the square with his aide-de-camp, had
stopped beside the \VUgras{fountain jet} \textsuperscript{(49)} of the
\VUgras{fountain} \textsuperscript{(48)} to watch the \VUgras{parade}.

%%K t14-14-2 p
\VUgras{The staff officers} \textsuperscript{(46)}, the \VUgras{colonel} and the
\VUgras{lieutenant colonel} saluted him with their swords.
\VUgras{The majors} were at the head of their \VUgras{battalions}
\textsuperscript{(47)} and each \VUgras{captain} commanded his \VUgras{company}, while the
\VUgras{lieutenants}, \VUgras{second lieutenants} and
\VUgras{non-commissioned officers} took their usual places beside their
men.

%%K t14-15-1 p
15. --- Many passers-by had gathered at the \VUgras{crossroads} \textsuperscript{(50)}; the
shopkeepers --- the \VUgras{umbrella seller} \textsuperscript{(51)}, the \VUgras{dyer}
\textsuperscript{(53)}, the \VUgras{gunsmith} \textsuperscript{(54)} --- came out to see the
\VUgras{soldiers}. Every vehicle --- \VUgras{cabs} \textsuperscript{(52)},
\VUgras{private carriages} \textsuperscript{(57)} and the \VUgras{water cart}
\textsuperscript{(56)} as well --- drew up along the pavement.

%%K t14-tit-7 sub
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{Scenes in the street.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t14-15-2 p
A \VUgras{commercial traveller} \textsuperscript{(55)} carrying his
\VUgras{sample cases} in his hand crossed the street briskly, and the people
sitting on the \VUgras{upper deck} \textsuperscript{(59)} of the \VUgras{omnibus}
\textsuperscript{(58)} turned round to enjoy the sight. A poor
\VUgras{street singer} \textsuperscript{(60)} with his \VUgras{barrel organ} \textsuperscript{(61)}
had to break off his \VUgras{song}, because the noise of the drums drowned his voice.

%%K t14-16-1 p
16. --- When the regiment came in front of the \VUgras{draper's shop} \textsuperscript{(64)},
the \VUgras{seamstresses} \textsuperscript{(63)} and the
\VUgras{tailors} \textsuperscript{(62)} left their \VUgras{sewing machines} and their
work to look out of the window. \VUgras{The shopkeeper} \textsuperscript{(65)}, who had just
put new \VUgras{suits} \textsuperscript{(66)} in his \VUgras{window display}, had to move the
rolls of \VUgras{cloth} \textsuperscript{(67)} and \VUgras{linen} set out on the pavement,
near the \VUgras{drain opening} \textsuperscript{(68)}, for fear the crowd would knock them
over; even an old \VUgras{pedlar} \textsuperscript{(69)}, who had a caretaker haggling with
him over some stockings, had to step into a hallway to finish his sale.

%%K t14-16-2 p
We went with the regiment as far as the barracks \VUgras{and}, very pleased with our day, came
home at dinner time.

%%K t14-tit-8 sub
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{Various trades and professions.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t14-17-1 p
17. --- The next day my father offered to take us round the printing works of the local paper.
We accepted enthusiastically.

%%K t14-17-2 p
The \VUgras{printer} is also a \VUgras{bookseller} \textsuperscript{(70)},
\VUgras{publisher}, \VUgras{stationer} and \VUgras{bookbinder}. He has put on
the first floor the \VUgras{editorial office} \textsuperscript{(71)} of the paper, and also
the \VUgras{engraving room}, where the \VUgras{lithographers} \textsuperscript{(72)} and the
\VUgras{copper engravers} \textsuperscript{(73)} work, and finally the composing room, where
the \VUgras{compositors} \textsuperscript{(74)} are. The \VUgras{press room}
\textsuperscript{(75)} proper, the \VUgras{printing presses} and the various machines, are on
the ground floor.

%%K t14-tit-9 sub
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{John asks a great many questions.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t14-18-1 p
18. --- On leaving the printing works, John stopped, greatly puzzled, in front of a small
glass box mounted on a pillar opposite the
\VUgras{haberdasher's} \textsuperscript{(77)}, and asked what it was for. My father
explained that it was a \VUgras{fire alarm} \textsuperscript{(76)}. Indeed everything in the
town was a wonder to my friend, from the plain
\VUgras{drinking fountain} \textsuperscript{(78)} and the light
\VUgras{delivery tricycle} \textsuperscript{(80)} ridden by a \VUgras{page boy}
\textsuperscript{(79)} in livery, to the \VUgras{mail van} \textsuperscript{(81)}.

%%K t14-19-1 p
19. --- While my father left us for a moment to speak to the
\VUgras{tax collector} \textsuperscript{(83)}, who had stopped to talk with a
\VUgras{barrister} \textsuperscript{(82)} and a \VUgras{notary} \textsuperscript{(84)}, we amused
ourselves following the traffic in the street. The most varied people passed before us: a
\VUgras{pastry cook's boy} \textsuperscript{(85)}, carrying a splendid \VUgras{pie} in a
basket, came behind a \VUgras{clothes dealer} \textsuperscript{(86)}; a \VUgras{lamplighter}
\textsuperscript{(87)} was talking with a
\VUgras{gas fitter} \textsuperscript{(88)}; a \VUgras{nun} \textsuperscript{(89)} holding a little
orphan girl by the hand was going back to her convent.

%%K t14-tit-10 sub
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{On our way home.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t14-20-1 p
20. --- Just as my father rejoined us, John burst out laughing at the grimy faces and the odd
get-up of two \VUgras{chimney sweeps} \textsuperscript{(91)} black with soot.

%%K t14-21-1 p
21. --- I wanted to buy a plant for my mother from the \VUgras{florist}
\textsuperscript{(92)}, but my father pointed out that it would be very heavy to carry and
that it would be better to get some \VUgras{oranges}. We went up to the \VUgras{stallholder}
\textsuperscript{(94)}, and when the \VUgras{governess} \textsuperscript{(93)}, who was
choosing some for her young charge, had finished her purchase, we were served.

%%K t14-22-1 p
22. --- On our way home we met several people we knew: an old friend of my family, leaning on
the arm of her \VUgras{lady companion} \textsuperscript{(95)}, the
\VUgras{engineer} \textsuperscript{(96)} of roads and bridges, and one of my cousins
with her \VUgras{nursemaid} \textsuperscript{(98)}.

%%K t14-22-2 p
Then we passed my mother's \VUgras{milliner} \textsuperscript{(97)} and two
\VUgras{monks} \textsuperscript{(99)} who were strangers to the town, and who came up to
ask my father the way to the station.
