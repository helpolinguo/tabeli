%%K t07-tit-2 sub
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{The village in winter.}
\VUsaut{4.0mm}

%%K t07-c1-01-1 p
1. --- During the New Year holidays I went with my father to Newtown, a
small village where he had some business to see to.

%%K t07-c1-02-1 p
2. --- We took the \VUgras{stagecoach} \textsuperscript{(11)} that runs between the
town and that village; but as it was very cold, the journey in so
uncomfortable a vehicle was not at all pleasant.

%%K t07-c1-03-1 p
3. --- So we were truly glad when we saw the \VUgras{driver} pull up his
coach in the square, in front of the \VUgras{inn} \textsuperscript{(2)} of the
\textit{White Bear}. The \VUgras{innkeeper} \textsuperscript{(4)} was waiting for us
on his doorstep, quietly smoking his \VUgras{pipe}.

%%K t07-c1-03-2 p
He asked us in, and I did not need to be asked twice before settling
myself in front of the vine-cutting fire crackling in the hearth. Then I
cared nothing for the biting \VUgras{north wind} that made the old
\VUgras{sign} \textsuperscript{(3)} creak and carried off in black swirls the
\VUgras{smoke} \textsuperscript{(1)} coming out of the chimney.

%%K t07-c1-04-1 p
4. --- It was lunchtime. Without waiting any longer, we did full justice
to the plain but tasty meal that was served to us (*).

%%K t07-tit-3 sub
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{A few visits.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t07-c1-05-1 p
5. --- After lunch I went with my father, who is an insurance agent, to
call on his clients. First we visited the \VUgras{blacksmith} \textsuperscript{(5)},
who lives next to the inn. He was busy shoeing a horse. I admired the
strength of his mate, who held the horse's \VUgras{hoof} firmly against his
leather apron while the smith fastened the \VUgras{shoe} \textsuperscript{(6)} with
heavy hammer blows.

%%K t07-c1-06-1 p
6. --- Then we went to the village \VUgras{shoemaker} \textsuperscript{(7)}, old
Jacob. Although he had a magnificent \VUgras{boot} for a sign, he was making
do with resoling an old pair of worn-out shoes.

%%K t07-c1-06-2 p
The old man told us with a laugh: “In town I was a ‘shoemaker’ and I had
no customers. Here I am a ‘cobbler’ and there is plenty of work.”

%%K t07-c1-07-1 p
7. --- He talked to us about his neighbour the \VUgras{barber} \textsuperscript{(8)},
whose customers are all dissatisfied. His \VUgras{razors} are always
chipped and his \VUgras{scissors} never cut properly. But as he is the only
barber in the village, people naturally have no choice but to have him
shave them and cut their hair. He is a mean and disagreeable man besides.

%%K t07-c1-08-1 p
8. --- Fortunately the other \VUgras{neighbours} are not like him. The
\VUgras{wheelwright} \textsuperscript{(9)}, for instance, is a kind man, hard-working
and obliging. It is a pleasure to give him a cart to mend. His work is
always well finished.

%%K t07-c1-09-1 p
9. --- Old Jacob had no complaints either about the \VUgras{saddler}
\textsuperscript{(10)}, whom he sometimes helps to sew \VUgras{harness} when the work
is pressing.

%%K t07-c1-09-2 p
My father asked him about his family. “Everyone is well. My
granddaughter is at \VUgras{school} \textsuperscript{(12)}. Her \VUgras{teacher}
\textsuperscript{(13)} is very pleased with her; she is a good \VUgras{pupil}
\textsuperscript{(14)}. She is not like that rascal of a son of mine; he thinks of
nothing but playing. The \VUgras{schoolmaster} \textsuperscript{(15)} cannot get him
to learn a thing.”

%%K t07-tit-4 sub
\VUsaut{3.0mm}
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{Village gossip.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t07-c1-10-1 p
10. --- The old man then told us the village news. A new school was to be
built, for the one in the \VUgras{village hall} had grown far too small.
That would be easy enough, since it would be enough to buy part of the
\VUgras{miller's} garden. It would suit the poor man very well. Since the
cold weather began, the \VUgras{millstones} of the \VUgras{mill}
\textsuperscript{(16)} had not been able to grind the wheat, the \VUgras{wheel}
\textsuperscript{(17)} being caught in the \VUgras{ice} \textsuperscript{(25)} of the
\VUgras{stream} \textsuperscript{(23)}.

%%K t07-c1-11-1 p
11. --- “Speaking of building, have you seen our new \VUgras{church}
\textsuperscript{(18)}? It looks very picturesque under its coat of snow. The
\VUgras{crows} \textsuperscript{(19)}, who no longer recognise their old bell tower,
perch sadly on the big tree in the \VUgras{churchyard} \textsuperscript{(20)}.”

%%K t07-c1-12-1 p
12. --- The mention of the churchyard reminded the shoemaker of the people
my father had known who had died in the past year. “How many \VUgras{graves}
\textsuperscript{(21)}, my dear sir, have been added to the others under the
\VUgras{cypress} \textsuperscript{(22)}! Death took our old notary. The whole
village was at his \VUgras{funeral}.”

%%K t07-c1-13-1 p
13. --- “There is his successor, skating on the stream. He came in a
little while ago to have me mend the strap of his \VUgras{skate}
\textsuperscript{(26)}, which had broken.”

%%K t07-c1-14-1 p
14. --- “And there, on the bank of the stream, is the new
\VUgras{village constable} \textsuperscript{(27)}. He is a former gendarme and the
terror of the village rascals. They run off the moment they catch sight
of his \VUgras{gaiters} \textsuperscript{(29)} and his \VUgras{peaked cap}
\textsuperscript{(28)}.”

%%K t07-c1-15-1 p
15. --- “Ah! there is old Joseph bringing a \VUgras{cartload of faggots}
\textsuperscript{(30)} for the baker. --- Quite right, said my father, I recognise
his team of \VUgras{mules} \textsuperscript{(31)}. I need a word with him, and I shall
take the chance. Goodbye, old Jacob.”

%%K t07-c1-16-1 p
16. --- Just as we were going out, the village children were leaving
school and rushing on to the \VUgras{ice slide} \textsuperscript{(33)}, at the risk of
falling and breaking a limb in the \VUgras{tumble} \textsuperscript{(34)}. One of them
fetched his \VUgras{sledge} \textsuperscript{(32)} from the wheelwright's, sat one of
his friends in it, and set off at a run.

%%K t07-c1-17-1 p
17. --- Others rushed to a \VUgras{heap of snow} \textsuperscript{(37)} near the
\VUgras{bridge} \textsuperscript{(40)} and began pelting the \VUgras{snowman}
\textsuperscript{(39)} they had made the day before, which they had given a hat, a
pipe and a school satchel slung over its shoulder.

%%K t07-c1-18-1 p
18. --- They cared little for the freezing wind and the cold. Most of them
had not even a \VUgras{scarf} \textsuperscript{(35)}, and to warm themselves up after
the \VUgras{snowball} \textsuperscript{(38)} fight, a handful of piping hot
\VUgras{chestnuts} \textsuperscript{(42)} bought from old Jacqueline, the
\VUgras{seller}, who shivers with cold under her too thin \VUgras{shawl}
\textsuperscript{(43)}, was all they needed.

%%K t07-tit-5 sub
\VUsaut{3.0mm}
\VUcentre{12.0pt}{0}{\textit{Second scene.}}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t07-tit-6 sub
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{The farmhouse in spring.}
\VUsaut{4.0mm}

%%K t07-c2-01-1 p
1. --- For all the pleasures of winter, it is with deep joy that we greet
the return of \VUgras{spring}.

%%K t07-c2-01-2 p
What a cheering picture a farmyard makes in the evening, in the month of
May!

%%K t07-c2-02-1 p
2. --- On the horizon the \VUgras{sun} \textsuperscript{(1)} is slowly setting,
flooding the \VUgras{countryside} \textsuperscript{(3)} with its crimson
\VUgras{rays} \textsuperscript{(2)}. The busy \VUgras{ploughman} \textsuperscript{(6)} is
hurrying to plough his \VUgras{field} \textsuperscript{(4)}.

%%K t07-c2-02-2 p
With his \VUgras{plough} \textsuperscript{(5)} drawn by a sturdy \VUgras{horse}
\textsuperscript{(7)}, he traces the \VUgras{furrow} \textsuperscript{(8)} in which the
\VUgras{sower} \textsuperscript{(9)} will scatter by the handful the \VUgras{seed}
that will yield the golden ears.

%%K t07-c2-03-1 p
3. --- The \VUgras{mowers} \textsuperscript{(11)} with their scythes have cut the
grass of the meadow, the haymakers have dried the \VUgras{hay}
\textsuperscript{(10)} by turning it with \VUgras{pitchforks} \textsuperscript{(12)} and
rakes, and here they come home.

%%K t07-c2-03-2 p
Some walk in front of the heavy cart drawn by oxen, carrying their tools
on their shoulders. Others, lazier, have settled themselves comfortably
on the sweet-smelling hay.

%%K t07-c2-04-1 p
4. --- As they pass they greet in friendly fashion the \VUgras{gardener}
\textsuperscript{(16)}, busy in the \VUgras{orchard} \textsuperscript{(13)} pruning the
\VUgras{fruit trees} and grafting the \VUgras{pear trees} \textsuperscript{(14)}. The
\VUgras{plum trees} \textsuperscript{(15)} are in blossom, and in the
well-manured \VUgras{vegetable garden} \textsuperscript{(17)} the vegetables,
cabbages and lettuces are already doing well.

%%K t07-c2-04-2 p
On the low wall, a handsome \VUgras{peacock} \textsuperscript{(18)} spreads its tail
to show off its magnificent feathers.

%%K t07-tit-7 sub
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{The farmyard.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t07-c2-05-1 p
5. --- The doors of the \VUgras{stable} \textsuperscript{(19)} are open, and one can
see the hayrack and the \VUgras{bedding straw} \textsuperscript{(23)} of the
\VUgras{mare} \textsuperscript{(21)} that the \VUgras{groom} \textsuperscript{(20)} has just
unharnessed. The \VUgras{foal} \textsuperscript{(22)}, so comical on its long legs,
follows its mother, frisking about.

%%K t07-c2-06-1 p
6. --- Near the \VUgras{well} \textsuperscript{(29)} the \VUgras{cows} \textsuperscript{(25)}
go to drink from the wooden \VUgras{trough} \textsuperscript{(26)} that serves them
for a drinking place. The \VUgras{calf} \textsuperscript{(24)} takes the chance to
suckle, and the \VUgras{ox} \textsuperscript{(27)}, catching the good smell of the
fodder, lows cheerfully and proudly lifts its head with its powerful
\VUgras{horns} \textsuperscript{(28)}.

%%K t07-c2-07-1 p
7. --- Everyone is at work. The \VUgras{maidservant} \textsuperscript{(32)} is
holding the \VUgras{rope} \textsuperscript{(31)} of the well. She is drawing water
in a \VUgras{bucket} \textsuperscript{(30)} to give the animals to drink. Near the
\VUgras{barn} \textsuperscript{(33)} a man is raking up the loose straw, and in front
of the door of the \VUgras{farmhouse} \textsuperscript{(34)} an old
\VUgras{spinning woman} \textsuperscript{(35)}, with her spinning wheel and her
\VUgras{distaff}, spins the \VUgras{flax} or the \VUgras{hemp} as in days gone
by.

%%K t07-tit-8 sub
\VUsaut{3.0mm}
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{The farmer.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t07-c2-08-1 p
8. --- The \VUgras{farmer} \textsuperscript{(36)} directs all this work and gives his
men their orders. He tells them to put under cover the \VUgras{harrow}
\textsuperscript{(40)} and the \VUgras{pickaxe} \textsuperscript{(39)} that have been left
near the \VUgras{kennel} \textsuperscript{(38)} of the \VUgras{dog} \textsuperscript{(37)}.

%%K t07-c2-09-1 p
9. --- His shouts startle a \VUgras{pigeon} \textsuperscript{(41)}, which flies off at
full speed to the \VUgras{dovecote} \textsuperscript{(42)}, and the \VUgras{hens}
\textsuperscript{(43)}, which hurry back into the \VUgras{henhouse} \textsuperscript{(44)}.

%%K t07-c2-10-1 p
10. --- In front of the \VUgras{sheepfold} \textsuperscript{(48)}, here is the old
\VUgras{shepherd} \textsuperscript{(45)} bringing back his \VUgras{flock}
\textsuperscript{(49)}. Holding his \VUgras{crook} \textsuperscript{(46)}, which never
leaves him any more than his faded \VUgras{smock} \textsuperscript{(47)} does, he
leads to the fold \VUgras{rams} \textsuperscript{(50)}, \VUgras{ewes} \textsuperscript{(53)},
\VUgras{lambs} \textsuperscript{(54)}, \VUgras{goats} \textsuperscript{(51)} and
\VUgras{kids} \textsuperscript{(52)}. His faithful \VUgras{dog} \textsuperscript{(55)} Argus
comes last and urges on the stragglers with his barking.

%%K t07-c2-11-1 p
11. --- All this noise and bustle does not disturb the calm of the good
\VUgras{dairy cow} \textsuperscript{(56)}, which rings its \VUgras{bell}
\textsuperscript{(57)} while it is milked in front of the door of the
\VUgras{cowshed} \textsuperscript{(58)}.

%%K t07-c2-12-1 p
12. --- She seems to understand that she must give her milk so that the
\VUgras{farmer's wife} \textsuperscript{(60)} can make \VUgras{butter} in the
\VUgras{churn} \textsuperscript{(61)}, or the excellent \VUgras{cheeses}
\textsuperscript{(64)} that are draining on the board laid across the \VUgras{tub}
\textsuperscript{(63)}.

%%K t07-c2-13-1 p
13. --- The whole \VUgras{dairy} \textsuperscript{(59)} is kept very clean by the
\VUgras{farmhand} \textsuperscript{(65)}, who has just washed the \VUgras{milk cans}
\textsuperscript{(62)} in the big bucket he is carrying.

%%K t07-tit-9 sub
\VUsaut{3.0mm}
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{The poultry yard.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t07-c2-14-1 p
14. --- In his heavy clogs he walks rather clumsily, and so puts to flight
the \VUgras{turkey} \textsuperscript{(68)}, the \VUgras{ducks} \textsuperscript{(66)}, the
\VUgras{drakes} \textsuperscript{(67)} and the \VUgras{geese} \textsuperscript{(69)}, which
open their \VUgras{beaks} \textsuperscript{(70)} wide and set up an anxious clamour.

%%K t07-c2-14-2 p
The \VUgras{cockerel} \textsuperscript{(71)}, more warlike, raises his red
\VUgras{comb} \textsuperscript{(72)}, shakes the feathers of his \VUgras{tail}
\textsuperscript{(73)} and lets out a ringing “cock-a-doodle-doo”.

%%K t07-c2-15-1 p
15. --- A \VUgras{mother hen} \textsuperscript{(74)} is pecking about on the
\VUgras{dung heap} \textsuperscript{(81)} with her \VUgras{chicks} \textsuperscript{(75)}.
The \VUgras{piglet} \textsuperscript{(78)} runs off to its mother, the huge
\VUgras{sow} \textsuperscript{(77)} we can see beside the pigsty.

%%K t07-c2-16-1 p
16. --- In their hutches, the \VUgras{rabbits} \textsuperscript{(79)} are eagerly
eating the tender \VUgras{grass} \textsuperscript{(80)} the farmer's daughter brings
them. Jenny is very fond of her rabbits, and every day, after fetching
the fresh \VUgras{eggs} \textsuperscript{(76)} from the henhouse, she comes to visit
her little friends.
