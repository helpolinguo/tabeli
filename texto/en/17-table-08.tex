\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{The Face of the Countryside.}
\VUsaut{3.0mm}

%%K t08-c1-01-1 p
1. --- With \VUgras{summer}, the countryside has changed its face once
more. The seed entrusted to the furrow has sprouted; a small green plant
has come up out of the ground and has borne its ear. The grain formed,
then ripened, and now nothing remains but to bring in the harvest.

%%K t08-c1-02-1 p
2. --- The \VUgras{wild flowers} are out. \VUgras{Cornflowers}
\textsuperscript{(1)}, \VUgras{poppies} \textsuperscript{(2)} and \VUgras{foxgloves}
\textsuperscript{(3)} set against the even tint of the cornfields the cheerful
contrast of their fresh \VUgras{blooms}.

%%K t08-c1-03-1 p
3. --- The fruit trees have put out their leaves; the fruit has ripened.
The little \VUgras{cherry tree} \textsuperscript{(4)} is laden with fine
\VUgras{cherries} \textsuperscript{(5)}, which the children pick and eat with
delight.

%%K t08-c1-04-1 p
4. --- The flock can now spend the whole day out of doors.

%%K t08-c1-04-2 p
The \VUgras{lambs} \textsuperscript{(6)} have grown and become fine \VUgras{ewes}
\textsuperscript{(7)} and fine \VUgras{rams} \textsuperscript{(8)} with thick fleeces.
They graze peacefully on the \VUgras{grass} of the \VUgras{pasture}
\textsuperscript{(9)}, dotted with \VUgras{daisies}.

%%K t08-c1-04-3 p
Soon these animals will be sheared for their \VUgras{wool}, from which the
cloth of our clothes is made.

%%K t08-tit-2 sub
\VUsaut{5.0mm}
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{The Herdsman.}
\VUsaut{3.4mm}

%%K t08-c1-05-1 p
5. --- The \VUgras{herdsman} \textsuperscript{(10)} does not seem to pay them much
attention. His only concern is the storm passing on the horizon, and the
\VUgras{shepherd} \textsuperscript{(13)}, raising his \VUgras{flask} \textsuperscript{(14)}
to his lips, seems more carefree still.

%%K t08-c1-06-1 p
6. --- The heat is oppressive; for the \VUgras{dog} \textsuperscript{(15)} comes to
cool himself too; he laps the fresh water of the \VUgras{stream}
\textsuperscript{(16)} and startles a poor little \VUgras{frog} \textsuperscript{(17)} that
had been crouching in the grass on the bank. It leaps into the clear
water where the \VUgras{water lilies} \textsuperscript{(18)} are in bloom.

%%K t08-c1-07-1 p
7. --- A \VUgras{wagtail} \textsuperscript{(19)} is bathing beside the little
\VUgras{spring} \textsuperscript{(20)} that gushes out between two rocks and hides
under the \VUgras{thorny scrub} \textsuperscript{(21)} and the
\VUgras{hawthorn bushes} \textsuperscript{(22)}.

%%K t08-tit-3 sub
\VUsaut{5.0mm}
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{The Harvest.}
\VUsaut{3.4mm}

%%K t08-c1-08-1 p
8. --- For country children, the corn harvest is a very entertaining time.
They turn merry \VUgras{somersaults} \textsuperscript{(24)} on the
\VUgras{straw heaps} \textsuperscript{(23)}, they help the \VUgras{harvesters}
\textsuperscript{(26)}, and while these mow the \VUgras{cornfield} \textsuperscript{(27)}
with their \VUgras{scythes} \textsuperscript{(25)}, well sharpened on the
\VUgras{whetstone} \textsuperscript{(30)}, they gather the corn and tie it into
\VUgras{sheaves} \textsuperscript{(31)} or \VUgras{small bundles} \textsuperscript{(32)}.

%%K t08-c1-09-1 p
9. --- Sometimes the biggest of them are allowed to cut with a
\VUgras{sickle} \textsuperscript{(12)} the \VUgras{golden stalks} \textsuperscript{(28)}
that bear the heavy \VUgras{ears} \textsuperscript{(29)} full of grain; and the
little ones, while watching the \VUgras{cattle} \textsuperscript{(33)} grazing in
the \VUgras{meadow} \textsuperscript{(34)} in the shade of the big \VUgras{lime tree}
\textsuperscript{(35)}, catch the beautiful \VUgras{butterflies} in their
\VUgras{net} \textsuperscript{(36)}.

%%K t08-c1-09-2 p
Some even climb on to the \VUgras{cart} \textsuperscript{(37)} to arrange the
sheaves that are handed up to them on a \VUgras{pitchfork} \textsuperscript{(38)}.

%%K t08-c1-10-1 p
10. --- All over the \VUgras{plain} \textsuperscript{(39)}, where the \VUgras{larks}
\textsuperscript{(41)} take flight, there is a great bustle. Everyone is busy
with the harvest. But while the poor go on using the old tools ---
\VUgras{scythes, sickles} and \VUgras{flails} \textsuperscript{(40)} --- the
wealthy landowners have their wheat or their \VUgras{rye} \textsuperscript{(43)} cut
with a \VUgras{reaping machine} \textsuperscript{(42)}. Then, without having to
carry the sheaves to the barn, great \VUgras{ricks} \textsuperscript{(44)} are built
on the spot.

%%K t08-c1-11-1 p
11. --- Then a \VUgras{threshing machine} \textsuperscript{(47)} is brought up,
driven by a \VUgras{portable engine} \textsuperscript{(45)} through a
\VUgras{driving belt} \textsuperscript{(46)}, and in this way the grain is
separated from the \VUgras{straw} without leaving the field where it was
sown.

%%K t08-tit-4 sub
\VUsaut{5.0mm}
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{The Storm.}
\VUsaut{3.4mm}

%%K t08-c1-12-1 p
12. --- Violent \VUgras{storms} \textsuperscript{(53)} often break at harvest time.
First the rumble of the \VUgras{thunder} is heard far off; a strong
\VUgras{west wind} \textsuperscript{(54)} gets up and bends the biggest trees of the
\VUgras{wood} \textsuperscript{(49)} until they groan. Black \VUgras{clouds}
\textsuperscript{(56)} storm the sky; they are crossed by the blinding zigzags of
the \VUgras{lightning} \textsuperscript{(55)}. The \VUgras{rain}, and sometimes the
\VUgras{hail}, begins to fall, flattening the crop that is ready to be cut.

%%K t08-c1-13-1 p
13. --- Often the lightning sets a building on fire. Last year, for
instance, the roof of the \VUgras{windmill} \textsuperscript{(50)} was burnt to
ashes and two of its \VUgras{sails} \textsuperscript{(51)} were shattered.

%%K t08-c1-14-1 p
14. --- After a few hours, calm returns. A brilliant \VUgras{rainbow}
\textsuperscript{(52)} appears on the horizon, and nature takes up again the life
that had been interrupted for a while. The country people, who had taken
shelter in the \VUgras{huts} \textsuperscript{(48)}, go back to work and set about
bravely repairing the damage they have just suffered.

%%K t08-tit-5 sub
\VUsaut{6.0mm}
\VUcentre{13.2pt}{90}{\textit{Second scene.}}
\VUsaut{3.6mm}

%%K t08-tit-6 sub
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{Hunting. --- The Grape Harvest.}
\VUsaut{1.4mm}
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{Fishing.}
\VUsaut{4.0mm}

%%K t08-c2-01-1 p
1. --- Towards the end of \VUgras{August} or the middle of
\VUgras{September}, depending on the region, the shooting season opens.
The first rays of the sun have hardly brought the \VUgras{lizards}
\textsuperscript{(2)} out of their holes before the \VUgras{sportsman}
\textsuperscript{(5)} sets out with his \VUgras{dogs} \textsuperscript{(4)}.

%%K t08-c2-02-1 p
2. --- He has put on his stout \VUgras{shooting suit} \textsuperscript{(9)} of
thick cloth, and leather \VUgras{gaiters} that will let him walk through
the wet grass where the \VUgras{toads} \textsuperscript{(1)} hide; he has taken his
\VUgras{gun} \textsuperscript{(6)} and his \VUgras{game bag} \textsuperscript{(10)}, and
off he goes.

%%K t08-c2-03-1 p
3. --- The eagerness of the chase brings him into the middle of a vineyard
where the grape harvest is in full swing. The winegrower's children, who
usually watch the goats by the roadside, are letting them graze where
they will today, and after tying to a stake an old \VUgras{billy goat}
\textsuperscript{(3)} who would certainly take advantage of his freedom to run
off, they too are taking their share of the fun. One takes a bunch of
grapes from a \VUgras{grape basket} \textsuperscript{(12)} and amuses himself by
squeezing the \VUgras{juice} \textsuperscript{(14)} into a \VUgras{cup}
\textsuperscript{(13)} to make must (unfermented wine). Another is crowning
himself with a \VUgras{wreath of leaves} \textsuperscript{(15)} he has just plaited.
Near them, bold \VUgras{sparrows} \textsuperscript{(16)} come right up to the press
to steal the grapes.

%%K t08-c2-04-1 p
4. --- While the children amuse themselves, the men work. The
\VUgras{grape pickers} \textsuperscript{(18)} carry the grapes down to the cellar in
\VUgras{back baskets} \textsuperscript{(17)}; a \VUgras{cooper} \textsuperscript{(19)} mends
the \VUgras{casks} \textsuperscript{(20)}, checks that the \VUgras{staves}
\textsuperscript{(22)} and the \VUgras{heads} \textsuperscript{(21)} are sound, and
replaces the broken \VUgras{hoops} \textsuperscript{(23)}. It was he too who made
the great \VUgras{vats} \textsuperscript{(25)} into which the \VUgras{grapes}
\textsuperscript{(26)} are poured to ferment.

%%K t08-c2-05-1 p
5. --- When the fermentation is over, the wine is drawn off and the
remains are put on the \VUgras{press} \textsuperscript{(28)}; by tightening the
great \VUgras{screw} \textsuperscript{(29)} little by little, the juice is squeezed
out and runs into a \VUgras{tub} \textsuperscript{(32)}, yielding still more
\VUgras{wine} \textsuperscript{(31)}.

%%K t08-tit-8 sub
\VUsaut{6.0mm}
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{Beer and Cider.}
\VUsaut{3.4mm}

%%K t08-c2-06-1 p
6. --- Up the wall of the \VUgras{cellar} \textsuperscript{(27)} climbs a branch of
\VUgras{hops} \textsuperscript{(30)}. Here it is only an ornamental plant, but in
some countries, where the vine is very rare, hops are grown to make
\VUgras{beer}.

%%K t08-c2-07-1 p
7. --- The little \VUgras{apple tree} \textsuperscript{(33)} is laden with apples
that will make excellent \VUgras{cider} when they are ripe. In their
\VUgras{cage} \textsuperscript{(34)} the \VUgras{canary} \textsuperscript{(35)} and the
\VUgras{goldfinch} \textsuperscript{(36)} look with envious eyes at the sparrows
frisking about in freedom.

%%K t08-c2-08-1 p
8. --- As far as the eye can see, on the slope of the hills, stand rows of
\VUgras{vine stakes} \textsuperscript{(40)} supporting the magnificent
\VUgras{vine stocks} \textsuperscript{(39)}, heavy with \VUgras{bunches}.

%%K t08-c2-08-2 p
All year long the \VUgras{winegrower} \textsuperscript{(42)} has worked to tend his
\VUgras{vineyard} \textsuperscript{(38)}, and now he is rewarded for his pains with
a fine crop. The \VUgras{women pickers} \textsuperscript{(41)} fill the vats set on
the cart drawn by \VUgras{mules} \textsuperscript{(43)}, and everyone is cheerful.

%%K t08-tit-9 sub
\VUsaut{5.0mm}
\VUpk{10.2pt}{40}{Fishing.}
\VUsaut{3.4mm}

%%K t08-c2-09-1 p
9. --- The same is true of the \VUgras{anglers} \textsuperscript{(44)}, who came at
first light to settle on the water's edge with their \VUgras{fishing rods}
\textsuperscript{(45)}.

%%K t08-c2-09-2 p
The \VUgras{fish} \textsuperscript{(47)} take the hook as soon as they drop their
\VUgras{line} \textsuperscript{(46)} into the water, and they have hardly time to
renew the \VUgras{bait} before a new victim is wriggling at the end of the
line.

%%K t08-c2-09-3 p
Still, the \VUgras{net fisherman} \textsuperscript{(48)}, who casts his net from his
\VUgras{boat} \textsuperscript{(49)} into the middle of the \VUgras{river}
\textsuperscript{(50)}, catches a good many more.

%%K t08-c2-10-1 p
10. --- Autumn is the season for good outings: on foot, on
\VUgras{horseback} \textsuperscript{(52)}, by \VUgras{bicycle} \textsuperscript{(53)}, by
\VUgras{car} \textsuperscript{(54)}. The \VUgras{roads} \textsuperscript{(51)} are clean and
people make the most of the last fine days, for already the
\VUgras{swallows} \textsuperscript{(56)} are gathering on the roofs to fly off to
warmer countries. The leaves of the old \VUgras{walnut tree} \textsuperscript{(57)}
are turning yellow, the \VUgras{walnuts} are falling, and the tall
\VUgras{trees} standing in a \VUgras{clump} \textsuperscript{(58)} on the
\VUgras{high ground} \textsuperscript{(59)} will soon be bare.

%%K t08-c2-11-1 p
11. --- The \VUgras{sun} \textsuperscript{(60)} rises later, the days grow shorter, a
fairly sharp chill begins to be felt in the \VUgras{morning}, and already
flights of \VUgras{woodcock} \textsuperscript{(61)} are leaving our inhospitable
climate.
